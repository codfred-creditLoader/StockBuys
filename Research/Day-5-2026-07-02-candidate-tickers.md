### 1. NVDA — NVIDIA Corporation
**Theme:** AI / Semiconductors
**Current Price:** ~$200
**Analyst Average Target:** $299 (about 49% upside)
**Consensus:** 73 Buys, 1 Hold, 0 Sells

**What they do:**
NVIDIA makes the specialized chips (GPUs) that are required to train and run AI systems. Think of them as the engine manufacturer in the AI gold rush — every company building AI, from startups to Google and Microsoft, needs NVIDIA chips to do it. Their most important product right now is called the H100/H200/Blackwell GPU, which costs $25,000–$40,000 per chip and is backordered for months.

**Growth:**
- Full fiscal year 2026 revenue: **$215.9 billion (+65% year-over-year)** — one of the largest revenue increases ever recorded by a company this size
- Most recent quarter (Q1 fiscal 2027): **$81.6 billion in a single quarter (+85% YoY)**, with data center revenue alone at $75.2 billion
- Earnings forecast to grow **24% per year** for the next 3 years

**Main risk:**
The stock already prices in a lot of good news. If major customers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon) slow their AI chip purchases — even temporarily — the stock will fall hard and fast. US export restrictions on chips sold to China have also cost NVIDIA billions. And at some point, competitors (AMD, Intel, in-house chips from Google and Apple) could erode their market share.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
NVIDIA isn't a speculative bet — it's a company generating real, massive cash flows from an indispensable product. With AI capital spending projected to top $5 trillion globally through 2030, this is the company making the tools everyone must buy. The risk is valuation, not business quality.

### 2. TSM — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company
**Theme:** Semiconductors / AI Infrastructure
**Current Price:** ~$452 (dropped 6.98% on July 2, 2026)
**Analyst Average Target:** ~$550+ (consensus "Strong Buy")

**What they do:**
TSMC doesn't design chips — it manufactures them for everyone else. NVIDIA designs the chip; TSMC actually builds it. Apple's iPhone chips, NVIDIA's GPUs, AMD's processors, Google's custom AI chips — they're all made at TSMC's factories in Taiwan. They control roughly 60% of the global contract chip manufacturing market and over 90% of the most advanced chips (below 7 nanometers). There is no realistic substitute for them.

**Growth:**
- Q1 2026 revenue: **$35.9 billion (+40.6% year-over-year)**
- Net income in Q1 2026: **+58.3% year-over-year**
- Full-year 2026 revenue growth projected at **30%+ in USD terms**
- Gross margin: 66.2% — which is extraordinarily high for a manufacturer
- AI and high-performance computing now represent **61% of total revenue**, up from about 40% two years ago

**Main risk:**
Taiwan. The geopolitical risk between China and Taiwan is the biggest single threat — if China were to blockade or invade Taiwan, TSMC's operations would be disrupted and global chip supply would collapse. TSMC is building plants in Arizona and Japan to reduce this risk, but Taiwan remains their core. The drop on July 2 may reflect fresh Taiwan Strait news or general market pressure — worth monitoring.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
Every advanced chip in the world runs through TSMC. As AI spending accelerates, TSMC's order book grows automatically — without needing to win any new business relationships. The geopolitical discount in the stock price is also an opportunity: if tensions ease even slightly, the stock rerate upward quickly.

### 3. GOOGL — Alphabet (Google)
**Theme:** Cloud Infrastructure / AI
**Current Price:** ~$361 (July 1, 2026)
**Market Cap:** $4.3 trillion (second most valuable company in the world)
**Analyst implied upside:** ~20%

**What they do:**
Google is best known for search, YouTube, and Gmail — but for investors in 2026, the story is **Google Cloud**, the company's cloud computing division. Google Cloud lets businesses rent computing power, store data, and now run AI applications through Google's platform. Their AI model (Gemini) is being built directly into cloud services, making Google Cloud increasingly the platform businesses choose when they want to deploy AI.

**Growth:**
- Google Cloud Q1 2026 revenue: **$20 billion (+63% year-over-year)** — the fastest growth among the three major cloud platforms
- Google Cloud backlog (future contracted revenue): **$462 billion** (doubled in the past year)
- Alphabet's market cap has grown by over $2 trillion in the past 12 months

**Main risk:**
Google's core search business faces a genuine threat from AI chatbots (people asking ChatGPT instead of Googling). While Google has answered with its own AI (Gemini), the transition creates uncertainty. Additionally, antitrust pressure continues — the US Department of Justice has ongoing cases against Google's search and advertising dominance. And at $4.3 trillion market cap, there's less room for explosive percentage gains compared to smaller companies.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
Google Cloud growing at 63% with a $462B backlog makes Alphabet one of the most direct beneficiaries of the AI buildout, at a valuation more reasonable than pure AI plays. The company generates so much cash it can spend aggressively on AI infrastructure while still buying back shares. A sleeper pick in a group of hyped names.

### 4. PLTR — Palantir Technologies
**Theme:** AI Software / Defense
**Current Price:** ~$127 (July 1, 2026; up 9% that day; down ~27% YTD)
**Full-year 2026 revenue growth guided:** ~71%
**Key Catalyst:** Golden Dome missile defense ($185B program)

**What they do:**
Palantir builds AI-powered software that helps governments and large companies make sense of enormous amounts of data. Their original business was intelligence — the CIA and military used Palantir software to track terrorist networks. Today they sell to commercial businesses as well (supply chains, hospitals, financial institutions). Their newest product, called AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform), lets organizations plug their own data into AI models and get actionable decisions out.

**Growth:**
- Q1 2026 revenue: **$1.633 billion (+85% year-over-year)** — the fastest growth in company history
- Full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised to imply ~**71% growth**
- Named as a key software backbone of the **Golden Dome missile defense system**, a $185 billion US government program. Rosenblatt estimates **$18.2 billion in government revenue** flowing to Palantir through 2028 from this contract alone.
- July 1: Stock jumped 9% after a new sovereign-AI partnership with NVIDIA was announced

**Main risk:**
Valuation is extreme. Even after the YTD decline, PLTR trades at roughly 110 times forward earnings. For context, NVIDIA at its peak trades at about 35x earnings. If revenue growth slows even slightly from 71% to, say, 40%, the stock could easily fall 40-50% from this level. The stock is also quite volatile — it fell 27% in the first half of 2026 despite record revenue growth because early-year expectations were even higher. Palantir has also historically diluted shareholders with stock-based compensation.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
Few companies in the world are growing revenue at 85% at a $30B+ revenue run rate. The Golden Dome contract is potentially transformational — $18B over two years would nearly double their current revenue base. If you can tolerate extreme volatility and a stretched valuation, this is one of the most direct plays on AI software being used at the highest levels of government and corporate decision-making.

### 5. KTOS — Kratos Defense & Security Solutions
**Theme:** Defense Tech / Drones / Hypersonics
**Current Price:** ~$63.40
**Analyst Average Target:** $104.67 (+65% upside)
**Analyst consensus:** 19 Buys

**What they do:**
Kratos makes tactical drones (small unmanned aircraft used in combat), hypersonic test vehicles, rocket propulsion systems, satellite communications equipment, and microwave products for the military. Unlike defense giants like Lockheed Martin, Kratos is a pure-play on the *new* kind of warfare — cheap, expendable drones; AI-guided missiles; and space-based communication systems. These are exactly the categories driving defense budget growth worldwide.

**Growth:**
- Q1 2026 revenue: **$371 million (+22.6% year-over-year; +15.8% organic)**
- Unmanned systems segment (drones): **+30.9% organic growth** in Q1
- Defense rocket systems: **+45.8%** in Q1
- FY2026 guidance: **$1.7–1.76 billion in revenue**
- FY2025 total revenue: $1.35B (+18.5%); prior year: $1.14B

**Main risk:**
Kratos is priced for rapid growth. Any stumble in contract wins, production delays, or a US defense budget fight in Congress could hurt the stock. The stock is also already up ~72% year-to-date in 2026, meaning some of the easy gains may already be captured. At 65% analyst upside remaining, there's still a case — but the stock has had a big run.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
Drones and hypersonics are the fastest-growing categories in defense. Every military on Earth is buying more unmanned systems. Kratos doesn't build $2B aircraft — they build $2 million tactical drones, which means more contracts, faster production cycles, and more addressable customers. With 19 analyst Buy ratings and 65% average upside to target, this is one of the more supported growth stories in the portfolio.

### 6. AVAV — AeroVironment, Inc.
**Theme:** Defense Tech / Drones
**Current Price:** ~$235 (estimated post-earnings; jumped 20%+ from ~$196 on June 30, 2026)
**Analyst Average Target:** $284 (~21% additional upside from current)

**What they do:**
AeroVironment makes the small drones and loitering munitions that have become essential to modern warfare. Their Switchblade drone (a "kamikaze" missile-drone hybrid) has been used extensively in the Ukraine war and is in high demand globally. They also make the Puma and Raven small surveillance drones used by US special forces. Think of AVAV as the drone supplier to the frontlines — while Kratos does technology development, AVAV is already in mass production.

**Growth:**
This is where it gets dramatic. Q4 fiscal 2026 was an absolute blowout:
- Q4 revenue: **$641.6 million vs. $556M estimate — a massive beat**
- Year-over-year comparison: Q4 prior year revenue was just **$275 million** — meaning revenue more than doubled
- Full fiscal year 2026 revenue: **$1.98 billion (+141% year-over-year)**
- Funded backlog: **$1.2 billion (+65% year-over-year)**

FY2027 guidance is more conservative ($2.13–2.23B revenue), which is why the stock only jumped 20% instead of more — but that's still 7–13% growth on top of an already tripled revenue base.

**Main risk:**
The stock just ran 20%+ on earnings, so some of the good news is now priced in. The FY2027 guidance disappointed some analysts who were expecting even more (they modeled $3.84 EPS vs. company's $3.02–3.34 guidance). International drone sales also depend on US export approvals, which can be delayed. And revenue doubling in one year is extraordinary — it's unlikely to repeat at that pace.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
This is the most "validated" high-growth story in our list — not projections, but actual reported numbers. Revenue +141% with a real $1.2B backlog. Global demand for tactical drones is not slowing down. Ukraine, Taiwan, Middle East — every conflict drives more orders. The funded backlog alone gives good revenue visibility for FY2027.

### 7. CEG — Constellation Energy
**Theme:** Nuclear Power / Clean Energy / AI Infrastructure
**Current Price:** ~$248 (down ~18-20% YTD; 52-week range: $243–$413)
**Analyst Average Target:** $360 (+45% upside)

**What they do:**
Constellation Energy owns and operates the **largest fleet of nuclear power plants in the United States** — 21 reactors at 12 facilities. Nuclear plants generate electricity 24/7, 365 days a year, regardless of weather. That makes them uniquely valuable for AI data centers, which need guaranteed power at all hours. Constellation is in the business of selling clean, reliable baseload electricity under long-term contracts.

**Growth:**
- Has signed long-term power purchase agreements covering **5,650+ megawatts** of clean energy to tech customers including Microsoft, Amazon, and now Walmart (which signed its first-ever nuclear energy deal with Constellation)
- $3.9 billion in capital spending underway to increase nuclear output through "uprates" (squeezing more power from existing plants)
- Analyst consensus: 23 analysts, average Buy rating, $360 target (45% upside)

**Main risk:**
Nuclear has regulatory risk — plants require extensive federal and state approvals, and any safety incident at any US nuclear plant would likely hurt Constellation's stock regardless of whether their plants were involved. The stock is also down 18% YTD from elevated 2025 highs, partly because investors are waiting on final confirmation of several large contracts. At $248, it's near its 52-week low of $243.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
The stock is near its 52-week low while the business has never been stronger. The AI data center power demand story is real and growing. Nuclear is the only "always-on" carbon-free electricity source that can serve Microsoft, Google, and Amazon at scale. The 45% analyst upside at a depressed price makes CEG one of the most interesting risk/reward setups in the list.

### 8. LNG — Cheniere Energy
**Theme:** Natural Gas / LNG Exports
**Current Price:** ~$244 (as of June 29, 2026)
**Analyst Average Target:** $303 (+29% upside)
**Consensus:** Strong Buy from 23 analysts

**What they do:**
Cheniere Energy is the **largest exporter of liquefied natural gas (LNG) in the United States**. They take natural gas from US wells, cool it to -260°F until it becomes liquid, load it onto specialized tanker ships, and sell it to buyers in Europe, Japan, South Korea, and elsewhere. Because the US is now the world's largest LNG exporter, Cheniere is essentially the toll bridge between American gas supply and global demand.

**Growth:**
- Q1 2026 EPS: **$4.77 vs. $4.23 estimate (beat)**
- Q1 2026 revenue: **$5.87 billion (beat estimates)**
- Raised full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance to **$7.25–7.75 billion** (up from $6.75–7.25B)
- US LNG exports forecast at **16.3 billion cubic feet/day in 2026**, up from 14.9 bcf/d in 2025
- Long-term demand from European nations replacing Russian gas and from data centers projected to drive demand to **28+ bcf/day by 2030**

**Main risk:**
Natural gas prices are volatile and beyond Cheniere's control. A global economic slowdown reduces energy demand. A wave of new LNG export terminals coming online from Qatar, Australia, or Canada could increase competition. Cheniere's revenues are largely contracted (locked in through the 2040s), which limits the upside from gas price spikes but also protects the downside.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
LNG is now a global infrastructure story, not just an energy trade. Europe permanently restructured away from Russian gas after 2022 — US LNG fills that gap for decades. Data centers need natural gas to power their 24/7 operations. The contracted, predictable revenue through the 2040s gives Cheniere an unusual combination of growth AND stability. At 29% upside and "Strong Buy" from 23 analysts, it's the most conservative name on this list but arguably the lowest-risk.

### 9. NTLA — Intellia Therapeutics
**Theme:** Biotech / Gene Editing
**Current Price:** ~$16 (52-week range: $7.95–$28.25)
**Market Cap:** ~$2.2 billion (small cap)

**What they do:**
Intellia is one of the leading companies developing CRISPR gene editing treatments. CRISPR is a technology that allows scientists to precisely edit DNA inside the human body to fix genetic diseases. Intellia's lead program (lonvo-z) edits the gene responsible for causing hereditary angioedema (HAE) — a painful disease where patients can suddenly swell up in dangerous attacks. Their second program (nex-z) targets a heart condition called ATTR amyloidosis.

**Growth (Clinical Milestones):**
- Phase 3 trial of lonvo-z: **87% reduction in HAE attacks** — a landmark result published in the New England Journal of Medicine
- **BLA (drug approval application) filed with the FDA** for lonvo-z; PDUFA date (expected FDA decision): **November 2026** — this is a huge near-term catalyst
- Phase 3 trials for nex-z (ATTR amyloidosis) resumed after FDA lifted clinical hold; enrollment to complete in second half of 2026
- The company has cash funded through at least 2028 — so there's no immediate funding emergency

**Main risk:**
Intellia has NO approved products and NO revenue yet. Every dollar in the stock price is a bet on drug approvals. The FDA could reject lonvo-z in November 2026 — which would likely cut the stock 50%+. Even an approval creates risk: Will insurance companies pay for it? Can they manufacture it at scale? A failed clinical trial would similarly devastate the stock. This is a "moonshot" pick.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
Gene editing is the future of medicine — fixing diseases at the DNA level rather than managing symptoms with daily pills. Intellia has the strongest data in the field right now. If lonvo-z gets approved in November 2026, the stock would likely trade materially higher from a $16 base. With the 52-week high at $28.25, approval would likely push it through that level. For a small allocation, this represents the highest risk AND the highest potential reward on the list.

### 10. SMMT — Summit Therapeutics
**Theme:** Biotech / Cancer Treatment
**Current Price:** ~$14 (recent trading range $13–17; 52-week high: $30.98)
**Analyst Average Target:** $28.54 (+103% upside implied)

**What they do:**
Summit Therapeutics is developing ivonescimab, a cancer drug that attacks tumors using two mechanisms at the same time (a "bispecific" antibody). It blocks both PD-1 (a checkpoint that lets cancer hide from the immune system) and VEGF (a protein that helps tumors grow blood vessels). Hitting both targets at once has shown better results than hitting either alone. Their main focus is **non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)**, one of the most common and deadly cancers.

**Growth (Clinical Milestones):**
- HARMONi-6 trial: ivonescimab **cut the risk of death by 34%** in first-line squamous NSCLC — a meaningful clinical achievement
- Phase 2 colorectal cancer data: **70.8% objective response rate**, beating the historical standard of care
- **FDA PDUFA date: November 2026** for NSCLC approval — a major binary event only 5 months away
- 17 analysts with a Buy consensus and average $28.54 target (100%+ upside)

**Main risk:**
An interim miss in the PD-1xVEGF trial surprised analysts (referenced in one search result) and caused shares to tumble earlier this year. The PDUFA date in November is a double-edged sword — approval would likely push the stock toward analyst targets; rejection would crater it. Summit also has limited resources compared to big pharma, creating execution risk in manufacturing and commercialization if approved.

**Why it fits aggressive high-growth:**
Lung cancer is a massive market. If ivonescimab gets approved in November 2026, it enters a market currently dominated by Merck's Keytruda (pembrolizumab), which does $25+ billion in annual sales. Even a small slice of that market would be transformational for a company with a $2–3B market cap. At $14 with a 100%+ analyst upside, the reward scenario is substantial. Like NTLA, this is a high-risk/high-reward position appropriate for a small allocation.
