## The Big Story: A Historic Rotation

For the last two years (2023–2025), the stock market was essentially one trade: buy big tech and AI. That trade is **broadening** in 2026. Investors are rotating money out of mega-cap tech and into "real economy" sectors — energy, industrials, and consumer staples.

Here's the short version:
- **Small caps beating large caps** — small companies up ~23%, large-cap growth up only ~2%
- **Energy and industrials beating tech** — by a wide margin
- **Alphabet is the big exception** — up 60% YTD among the tech giants

This doesn't mean tech is dead. It means the easy money in "just buy Nvidia" has been squeezed, and investors are hunting for the next wave.

1. Trump's energy deregulation has unleashed US oil production (above 13 million barrels/day — record)
2. Geopolitical tensions keep oil prices elevated
3. Refiners and energy services companies (not just drillers) had surprisingly wide profit margins

## The HOT List: What's Been Winning

### 1. Energy Stocks — Up ~22-24% as a Sector

The winners inside energy aren't who you'd expect. The biggest moves came from **refiners** and **energy service companies**, not the big oil names.

- **Marathon Petroleum** — benefiting from wider gasoline/diesel margins, buying back billions in stock
- **Chevron** — projecting $12.5 billion in additional cash flow in 2026, Permian Basin + Hess acquisition
- **Constellation Energy** — clean energy play; closed a $26.6B acquisition of Calpine in early 2026, now the dominant US nuclear and clean energy company
- **Morningstar US Energy Index: +24.12%** vs the broader market's +11.22%

### 2. Industrials — Up ~16%

- **Caterpillar** was the biggest single contributor — infrastructure, construction, global demand
- Defense spending (more on this in Day 4) is boosting the industrial complex

### 3. Consumer Defensive — Up ~13%

- **Walmart** and **Costco** leading — people spend regardless of economic fear
- These are "sleep well at night" stocks, not high-growth, but they show investor caution about the economy

### 4. Alphabet (GOOGL) — Up ~60% YTD

This is the standout among Big Tech. While other tech giants treaded water, Google's stock roughly doubled year-over-year. Why?
- Strong AI product execution (Gemini improvements, AI Overviews in Search)
- YouTube ad revenue held up better than competitors
- Cloud revenue accelerating
- The stock was cheaper than peers heading into 2026

### 5. Small Caps — Up ~23%

The Russell 2000 (small company index) has outperformed the large-cap S&P 500 significantly. Small companies benefit most from:
- A stable economy (they're more sensitive to domestic growth)
- Rate stability (many carry floating-rate debt — no more hike fears help them)
- Less exposure to international trade/tariff issues

## The COLD List: What's Been Losing

### 1. Tech (Broad) — Flat to Slightly Negative

The Nasdaq and technology ETFs have largely underperformed in 2026. The mega-cap AI names (Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta) spent 2024-2025 going parabolic, and now investors are waiting to see if profits catch up to the hype.

- **Nvidia** — still the AI chip leader, but has had sharp pullbacks as investors wonder when the AI buildout spending translates to sustained profits
- **Microsoft** — large but flat; still spending aggressively on AI ($190B capex guided for 2026)

Big Tech is spending an almost unbelievable combined **$725 billion** on AI infrastructure in 2026 — up 77% from last year's record. That's money going OUT (expenses), not coming IN. Investors are watching nervously.

| Reddit (RDDT) | Down significantly | Post-IPO hype faded, monetization concerns |

### 3. Communications / Streaming

The broader media/telecom space struggled. Streaming wars have compressed margins. Ad revenue was uneven. Companies like Warner Bros. Discovery and Paramount continued their post-merger struggles.

## What This Means for Our $1,000

Three things stand out from today's analysis:

1. **Energy is the momentum trade right now** — but it's not the "aggressive high-growth" play Freddy is looking for. It's a cyclical winner. We'll note it but likely skip it for Day 7.

2. **Small caps are breaking out** — this is interesting. Smaller, faster-growing companies are finally getting attention after years of large-cap dominance. This is where explosive growth lives.

3. **Tech isn't dead, it's just resting** — the massive AI capex spending ($725B from just 4 companies) is not a sign of weakness. It's building the rails. The companies that *sell picks and shovels to the AI miners* may still be the best long-term play. We'll dig into that on Day 3.
