## The Federal Reserve and Interest Rates

The Fed held rates steady at **3.5% – 3.75%** at its June 17, 2026 meeting.

**New chair Kevin Warsh** is focused on price stability. He's not rushing to cut rates.

**What changed:** The Fed's dot plot (their forward guidance grid) now suggests:
- NO rate cuts expected in 2026 — a rate *hike* is even possible
- Cuts pushed out to 2027–2028
- Median rate projected at 3.8% by year-end 2026 (slightly above current level)

**Why this matters for investors:** Higher-for-longer rates are tough on high-growth stocks because companies that are burning cash and growing fast are valued based on future earnings. When interest rates are high, those future earnings are worth less in today's dollars. This is one reason tech and growth stocks have pulled back in recent weeks.

## Inflation: Still Hot

| Metric | Latest Reading |
|---|---|
| Headline CPI (May 2026) | **+4.2% year-over-year** — highest since April 2023 |
| Core CPI (ex-food & energy) | **+2.9% year-over-year** |
| Energy prices (12-month) | **+23.5%** — gasoline up 40.5% |

**What's driving it:** An energy shock caused by conflict with Iran has sent gas prices soaring. This pushed headline inflation back above 4% — a big deal because the Fed's target is 2%. Core inflation (stripping out food and energy) is more moderate at 2.9%, but that's still above target.

**April reading:** CPI was 3.8%. May jumped to 4.2%. The trend is going the wrong direction.

## The Economy: Solid But Watchful

- **GDP growth:** Expected around 2.5% for 2026 (Goldman Sachs estimate; consensus is ~2.1%)
- **Jobs market:** Still resilient — the Sahm Rule indicator (a recession signal) sits at just 0.10, far below the 0.50 danger zone
- **Recession risk:** New York Fed puts 12-month recession probability around 15%; other models at ~20%. Not high, but not zero
- **AI spending:** Massive capital investment in AI infrastructure is propping up growth. If that cools, recession risk rises

## Risk-On or Risk-Off Right Now?

**The short answer: Mixed — leaning risk-on overall, but with pockets of fear.**

- Goldman Sachs' Risk Appetite Indicator is in the **99th percentile** historically — investors are very willing to take risk overall
- Analysts expect **14–16% earnings growth** for S&P 500 companies in 2026 — very bullish
- Morgan Stanley targets S&P 500 at **7,500** (currently ~7,354, so modest upside in their view)

**But there are real warning signs:**
- Tech stocks are selling off (risk-OFF signal in that sector)
- Valuations are stretched — the top 10 stocks make up 40% of the S&P 500
- Inflation is re-accelerating, which could force the Fed to hike
- The Iran conflict adds geopolitical uncertainty
- Energy price shock is hitting consumers and businesses

**The result:** Money is rotating. Investors are moving OUT of high-flying tech/AI names and INTO more stable, dividend-paying sectors like utilities, consumer staples, and healthcare. This "defensive rotation" can punish growth stocks even in a broadly healthy market.

## Key Terms (Plain Language)

- **CPI:** Consumer Price Index — measures how much everyday goods cost. Up = inflation.
- **Core CPI:** Same thing but removes food and gas prices, which swing a lot. Tells a cleaner story.
- **Fed Funds Rate:** The interest rate banks charge each other. The Fed controls this to speed up or slow down the economy.
- **Dot plot:** A chart the Fed publishes showing where each member thinks rates will go. It's a signal, not a promise.
- **Risk-on:** Investors are feeling bold — they buy stocks, especially growth stocks.
- **Risk-off:** Investors get scared — they sell stocks and move into bonds, gold, or cash.
- **Defensive rotation:** Investors move from high-growth, volatile sectors into more stable, boring sectors.
