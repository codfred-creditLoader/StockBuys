## 1. BIOTECH

### What is biotech?
Biotech companies use biology and technology to develop drugs and therapies. They can be huge, established companies like Pfizer, or tiny startups with one experimental drug. The smaller ones are riskier but can also deliver massive gains — sometimes 200%+ in a single year if a drug trial succeeds.

### Why biotech is exciting right now

**The obesity/GLP-1 revolution is still in early innings.** Drugs like Ozempic and Wegovy (made by Novo Nordisk) have redefined what's possible in weight loss. The market for these drugs could hit $100 billion per year. Right now, 1-2 new obesity drug approvals are expected every year through 2027+. Every major pharmaceutical company is racing to get their version approved. This creates a long runway of growth.

**Gene therapy just hit its first major milestone.** For years, gene therapy (the idea of editing your DNA to cure a disease) has been more hype than reality. That changed in 2026. A company called Intellia Therapeutics got a CRISPR gene-editing drug through Phase 3 trials — the highest bar before FDA approval. Their drug for hereditary angioedema reduced attack rates by 87%. This opens the door to a wave of gene-based treatments over the next decade.

**AI drug discovery is going clinical.** AI tools that predict how molecules interact with the body have been generating buzz for 5+ years. In 2026, the first AI-designed drugs started showing up in late-stage trials. The buzzword has become reality.

**Big pharma is on a buying spree.** Companies like Pfizer, Roche, AbbVie, and Amgen spent $65+ billion acquiring smaller biotechs in 2025 alone — the most since the COVID era. Why? Their own top-selling drugs are losing patent protection, so they're buying growth. This M&A activity keeps a floor under smaller biotech stock prices, because any one of them could be the next acquisition target.

### Real risks in biotech
- **Trial failures are sudden and brutal.** A drug can fail Phase 3 even after great Phase 2 results. The stock can lose 50-80% overnight.
- **FDA rejections are unpredictable.** Even strong drugs get rejected for manufacturing or labeling issues.
- **Dilution.** Small biotechs constantly raise money by selling new shares, which reduces the value of existing shares.
- **Valuation.** After strong 2025-2026 gains, many biotechs are priced for perfection.

### Bottom line on biotech
The sector is in a genuine growth cycle driven by obesity drugs, gene therapy breakthroughs, and $65B+ in M&A. XBI has been the standout performer, up 40%+ just in early 2026. The risk is real — but so is the upside for a 2-5 year hold. Better used as an ETF (XBI) than individual stocks for most investors, unless you're willing to track specific pipeline events closely.

## 2. ENERGY: CLEAN (Solar, Wind, Nuclear, Hydrogen)

### The structural shift happening right now

The biggest surprise in clean energy in 2026 is that the demand driver isn't environmental policy — it's data centers and AI. Data centers need massive amounts of electricity, 24/7. The International Energy Agency projects global data center electricity demand to roughly **double to 945 TWh by 2030**. Utilities can't build enough natural gas plants fast enough to fill that gap. So solar + battery storage is being signed into long-term contracts as the fastest way to add power.

This has changed the clean energy story from "depends on government subsidies" to "the market needs it."

### Policy is mostly fine
The Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), passed in 2022, gave massive tax credits to clean energy companies. There were fears these would be cut. While some adjustments happened, the **major manufacturing credits and domestic investment incentives stayed mostly intact.** This triggered a big relief rally in clean energy stocks in 2025.

### Clean energy performance in 2026
- **ICLN (iShares Global Clean Energy ETF):** +27% YTD 2026
- **TAN (Invesco Solar ETF):** +28% YTD 2026
- **PBW (Invesco WilderHill Clean Energy ETF):** +31% YTD 2026
- **URA (Global X Uranium & Nuclear ETF):** +70% over the trailing year

### Nuclear is having a major moment
AI companies like Microsoft, Google, and Amazon have signed nuclear power purchase agreements. Small modular reactors (SMRs) are under construction. Constellation Energy (CEG), which owns most of America's existing nuclear plants, has surged. The nuclear story is tied directly to the AI buildout — it provides always-on, carbon-free baseload power that solar/wind can't provide at night or during low-wind periods.

### Real risks in clean energy
- **Interest rate sensitivity.** Clean energy projects are financed with debt. Higher rates make projects less profitable. The Fed's recent cuts helped, but if inflation resurges, this sector gets hit.
- **Supply chain issues.** Chinese-made solar panels face tariffs; US manufacturing capacity is still ramping up.
- **Policy risk.** If IRA credits get cut, smaller companies lose their economics.
- **Grid connection delays.** Utilities are overwhelmed with connection requests. Projects can take years to get approved.

## 3. ENERGY: TRADITIONAL (Oil, Gas, Pipelines)

### The oil picture: complicated but interesting

Oil faces a supply headache. Non-OPEC countries (US, Guyana, Brazil) are expected to add over 1 million barrels per day of new production in 2026. OPEC+ has been cutting production to keep prices stable, but the cartel's discipline is imperfect. The result: oil prices have been pressured, and XLE (the big oil & gas ETF) faces some headwinds from that.

**However, XLE is still up ~21.6% in 2026 YTD** because oil company earnings are strong — they learned to be profitable at $60-70/barrel, and buybacks + dividends are attracting investors.

### Natural gas is the star of traditional energy

This is the real growth story in traditional energy right now:

**Why natural gas demand is exploding:**
1. **Data centers need gas turbines** to power their AI infrastructure. Gas plant construction costs surged 66% in the last two years because demand is so high.
2. **LNG exports are booming.** The US is now the world's largest LNG exporter. Exports are forecast to hit 16.3 billion cubic feet per day in 2026, up from 14.9 bcf/d in 2025. By 2030, data centers alone could use 6.1 bcf/d.
3. **Europe is replacing Russian gas** with US LNG permanently.

**Natural gas demand is so strong that LNG export projects and data center power plants are competing for the same gas supply.**

- **Oil price can drop fast.** OPEC+ compliance breaks, or a global recession slows demand — oil stocks follow.
- **Energy transition.** Over a 10-year horizon, fossil fuel demand growth may plateau.
- **Political & regulatory risk.** Permitting, pipeline approvals, LNG export authorizations can be revoked by a new administration.
- **Natural gas oversupply.** If too many projects come online at once, gas prices crash.

## 4. DEFENSE

### Why this is a true multi-year super-cycle

Defense spending is growing globally at a rate not seen since the Cold War. This isn't driven by one conflict — it's structural.

**By the numbers:**
- Global military spending hit **$2.887 trillion in 2025** — the 11th consecutive year of growth
- European NATO members grew spending by 14% in 2025 — the highest SIPRI has ever recorded
- For the first time in history, **all 31 NATO allies met the 2% of GDP target** in 2025
- At The Hague Summit in June 2026, NATO members **committed to 5% of GDP by 2035** — a transformational pledge
- US defense spending for FY2026: **$1.05 trillion** (+17% over FY2025)
- President Trump proposed **$1.5 trillion for FY2027** — Congress has not yet approved

**The drivers:**
- Ukraine war (no end in sight as of July 2026)
- Taiwan tensions (Taiwan's defense budget +14% to $18.2 billion)
- Middle East conflicts (Israel, Iran, proxy wars)
- China's military expansion across all domains
- Hypersonic missile threat requiring completely new defense systems

### The new tech angle: drones, AI, hypersonics

The most exciting growth in defense isn't tanks and ships — it's autonomous systems, AI, and hypersonic missiles. The US Army is accelerating acquisition of counter-drone systems and hypersonic interceptors. The Pentagon has committed $4.1 billion to hypersonic R&D.

The **Golden Dome** program (a $185 billion missile defense system) is a real catalyst — it combines space-based sensors, AI software, and interceptors to defend against hypersonic attacks. Palantir is a key software backbone of this program.

- **Political reversal.** The $1.5T Trump budget for 2027 still needs Congressional approval. An administration change or budget fight could slow spending.

- **Valuation risk.** Some names (KTOS +280% in a year) may be pricing in perfection.
- **Execution risk.** B-21, F-35 production has historically run behind schedule and over budget.

### Bottom line on defense
This is arguably the strongest structural growth story in this entire 7-day sprint. NATO rearmament is a decade-long spending commitment backed by formal pledges. The US budget grew 17% in one year. Drone and AI warfare is in its early innings. The best aggressive plays are AVAV and KTOS for drone pure-plays, PLTR for AI software (high risk/high reward), and XAR ETF for a diversified growth-tilted basket.

## 5. SMALL-CAP STOCKS

### What are small-caps?
Companies with market capitalizations roughly between $300 million and $2 billion. They're usually less well-known, covered by fewer analysts, and more volatile than large-cap stocks. But they can grow faster and are often acquisition targets.

The benchmark: **Russell 2000 index (ticker: IWM for the ETF).**

### Why small-caps could be a major winner right now

**1. Interest rates are falling — and small-caps feel it most.**
The Fed cut interest rates by 175 basis points (1.75%) from late 2024 through 2025, with the current target range at 3.50-3.75%. Small companies carry more debt relative to their size, and that debt is often variable-rate. Lower rates directly improve their earnings. The key insight: rate cuts take 12-18 months to show up in financial results. That means May-July 2026 is **precisely when small-cap earnings are starting to reflect those cuts** — giving them a fresh fundamental tailwind.

**2. Valuation gap is at a 25-year extreme.**
At the end of 2025, the Russell 2000 traded at a forward P/E of ~18x, while the S&P 500 was at ~26x. Morningstar's Q2 2026 data says small-caps are trading at a **17% discount to fair value** — the deepest discount of any US equity segment. Large-cap tech has been the only game in town for years; small-caps got left behind.

**3. Earnings growth is outpacing large-caps.**
Q1 2026 Russell 2000 earnings growth consensus: **44.9% year-over-year**. Bank of America forecasts small-cap earnings to grow 17% in 2026 vs. 14% for large-caps.

**4. M&A is heating up.**
US acquisitions over $100 million were up 25% by volume and 43% by value in early 2026. Small-caps are the targets — any holding in the Russell 2000 could be bought out at a premium.

### 2026 performance so far
- **IWM** surged +11.7% in April 2026 alone (strong recovery)
- Jefferies has a year-end Russell 2000 target of **2,825 — implying +14% from current levels**
- Headlines are calling this the "Great Rotation" — money moving from big tech into smaller companies

### Real risks in small-caps
- **Refinancing risk.** Some small companies took on debt when rates were near zero. Even at 3.5%, refinancing an old 1% loan at much higher rates is painful. "Zombie companies" that survived on cheap debt could fail.
- **Inflation resurgence.** Bank of America warns core PCE could end 2026 at ~3.0%, and if inflation re-accelerates the Fed pauses — that would hammer small-caps again.
- **Consumer discretionary exposure.** Small-cap consumer companies are vulnerable to tariff cost increases.
- **Less liquidity.** Harder to exit in a panic without moving the price.

### Bottom line on small-caps
The setup is historically attractive — deep valuation discount, rate cut tailwinds just starting to hit earnings, and M&A premiums as a bonus. IWM is the simple, broad play. For more aggressive exposure, focus on profitable small-caps in domestic industrials, AI-adjacent tech, or biotech within the small-cap universe. This is a theme worth keeping in mind as we narrow our final picks.
